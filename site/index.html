AI Adoption Signals

As businesses adopt AI, do attacks and outages rise with it?

Business AI use is climbing every month. So are many measures of cyber risk. This site checks whether they actually move together, using only official and public data that refreshes every week.

The short answer

Rising together is not the same as moving together

If two things both grow over time, they will always look linked. The real test is whether the month-to-month changes line up: in months when AI adoption jumps more, do attacks or outages jump more too? The table puts both numbers side by side.

ρ (rho) is Spearman's rank correlation: +1 means they always move up together, 0 means no pattern, −1 means opposite. "No reliable link" means the pattern in the changes could easily be chance (p ≥ 0.05) or is too small to matter (|ρ| < 0.1). Each comparison uses only the months both series cover.

1 · The baseline

How fast are businesses adopting AI?

Two independent measures. Ramp counts US businesses on its card and bill-pay platform that paid for AI tools that month (monthly since 2023). The Census Bureau asks a representative sample of about 1.2 million US employers every two weeks whether they used AI in the last two weeks.

2 · Cyber attacks

Software flaws that attackers are actually using

The US Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency (CISA) lists every vulnerability it has evidence of being exploited in real attacks: the Known Exploited Vulnerabilities (KEV) catalogue. It is the closest public, official, regularly updated record of real attacks.

    Method & sources

    How this works

    • Months. Everything is counted by calendar month from January 2023, and only finished months are used.
    • Two correlations. "Levels" compares the raw monthly values; "changes" compares month-to-month differences. Only the second can tell a real connection from two trends that just happen to share a direction.
    • Chance check. For the changes, we shuffle one series 5,000 times and report how often a shuffled version looks at least as linked (the p-value).
    • One fixed comparison. We compare same-month changes only and do not hunt through time lags, which would find patterns by chance.
    • Correlation is not cause. Even a reliable link would not show that AI causes attacks; both could respond to something else, such as overall growth in software and cloud use.
    • Known limits. KEV records when CISA confirmed exploitation, which can lag the first attack. Keyword matching in NVD will miss some AI flaws and include a few false matches. Status pages are written by the companies themselves, and their practices change. Ramp covers only its own customers. The Census question was reworded in November 2025, so only the new wording is used here.
